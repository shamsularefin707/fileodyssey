Sediment movement in a lowland river

R. Hossain and T. Alam, Valley Field Station

Abstract. We report two seasons of level and sediment readings from a lowland river and show that a new sandbank has changed the current along the eastern bank.

1 Introduction

The valley survey began before dawn on the first Saturday of spring. Three volunteers carried the measuring poles down to the old ferry landing while the fog was still lying on the water.1

The highest reading came from the bend below the mill, where the new sandbank has pushed the current against the eastern bank. The keeper says the change began after the winter floods.

2 Method

By mid-morning the sun had burned through and the river showed its true colour, a slow green that darkened under the alders. Every marker was read twice and written in pencil.

Lunch was eaten on the warm stones of the shelf, and the afternoon was spent walking north to the bridge, checking the markers that the spring storms had loosened.

3 Results

Nobody expected the count of herons to be so high. Eleven birds were seen in one stretch of reeds, more than in any survey since the records were first kept.2

4 Conclusion

In the evening the notes were typed up in the village hall. The figures will go to the county archive, where they join a series that goes back almost forty years.

Notes

1. Readings were taken at the same seven markers each time.

2. Heron counts are from the morning walk only.
