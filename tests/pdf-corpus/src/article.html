A morning on the river

The valley survey began before dawn on the first Saturday of spring. Three volunteers carried the measuring poles down to the old ferry landing while the fog was still lying on the water. The work is slow, careful and, on a good day, quietly beautiful.

Readings

By mid-morning the sun had burned through and the river showed its true colour, a slow green that darkened under the alders. Every marker was read twice and written in pencil.

The highest reading came from the bend below the mill, where the new sandbank has pushed the current against the eastern bank. The keeper says the change began after the winter floods. See the survey website for the full method.

What we found

  • The water rose at every marker.
  • A new sandbank formed below the mill.
  • Eleven herons nested in the reeds.

Lunch was eaten on the warm stones of the shelf, and the afternoon was spent walking north to the bridge, checking the markers that the spring storms had loosened.

Next steps

In the evening the notes were typed up in the village hall. The figures will go to the county archive, where they join a series that goes back almost forty years.
